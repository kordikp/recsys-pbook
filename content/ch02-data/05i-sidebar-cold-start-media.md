Every Friday, streaming platforms swallow tens of thousands of new tracks. Each arrives with **zero plays** — and a fan base that expects new music to find them *that day*. Item cold start here is not an edge case; it is the weekly heartbeat of the whole catalog.

The bridge is that a track carries an unusual amount of machine-readable content:

- **The audio itself.** Encoders extract tempo, energy, instrumentation, mood — a fingerprint that places the track among sonic siblings without a single listener. That is how a brand-new lo-fi instrumental lands in a focus playlist within hours of release.
- **Metadata and context.** Genre labels, the artist's existing audience, label, release notes. A new track by a known artist inherits a warm audience; a debut leans almost entirely on audio similarity.

Then comes the platform's superpower: **fast, dense feedback**. A track earns plays, skips, saves and playlist-adds within *hours* — the richest early signal of any domain (a skip at 0:07 says more than a missing click ever could). So the handoff from content to behavior, which takes days in a shop, can happen over a weekend.

Compare the tempo across domains: books wait days for purchases, jobs wait for applications, but music learns by Monday. **Same bridges, different clock speeds.**
