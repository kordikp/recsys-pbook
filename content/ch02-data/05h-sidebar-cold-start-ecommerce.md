A merchandiser uploads a new trail-running shoe on Monday morning. By Monday afternoon she is asking the classic question: *why doesn't it show up in "customers also bought"?*

Because that widget is **built from co-purchase patterns**, and a shoe nobody bought co-occurs with nothing. It is not being punished; it is statistically invisible.

What a well-run shop does instead, in roughly this order:

- **Attribute placements, immediately.** The shoe's category, brand, price band and description text slot it into "new in trail running" and "similar to items you browsed." These need no behavior at all — they run on what the product *is*.
- **A bounded exploration slice.** The shoe gets a few days of guaranteed impressions inside its category pages and recommendation shelves — enough to gather honest clicks, capped so a dud can't squat on prime real estate. Watch the early **click-through and add-to-cart rates**: they are the shoe's first honest reviews.
- **The handoff.** After the first dozens of purchases, co-behavior exists, and the shoe starts earning "customers also bought" placements on its own merit. The exploration slice quietly retires.

The pattern to remember: **content gets the item seen, exploration gets it measured, behavior gets it ranked.** A shop that skips the middle step ends up with a catalog where only last year's bestsellers ever surface.
